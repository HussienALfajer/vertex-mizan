# packages/db

Drizzle schema, migrations, row-level security, the database roles and the company context (ADR 0002, ADR 0003, ADR 0005, ADR 0008). PostgreSQL 17, node-postgres driver, snake_case columns from camelCase properties. Copied from Vertex Food's `packages/db` and adapted. The general machinery is proven on test-only fixture tables; S01 brought the first tables (people, companies, access, the audit logs), S02 the chart of accounts, dimensions and rates, S03 the ledger, its state and the posting function, S04 the open items behind the control accounts, S05 the event inbox and the posting-rule mapping.

## Layout
- `src/schema/<module>.ts`: one file per owning module, exported from `schema/index.ts`. A platform table never has a column named `company_id` (the convention test reads that as company scope): one that points at a company names it `scope_company_id` (the platform audit log: `subject_company_id`).
  - `access.ts`: Better Auth's tables (`users`, `accounts`, `sessions`, `verifications`, `two_factors`, `rate_limits`; property names are the field names its Drizzle adapter expects), `memberships`, `invitations` and the `membership_role` enum (S01).
  - `companies.ts`: `companies` (platform) and `company_settings` (company: the ledgers, fiscal year start and policy note, locked by `company_settings_lock()` once `books_started_at` is set). `SETTINGS_CURRENCY_CODES` and `SETTINGS_RATE_TYPES` are the money module's lists its checks accept (a test keeps them equal).
  - `audit.ts`: `platform_audit_entries` and `audit_entries` (company), both append-only.
  - `chart.ts` (S02): `chart_accounts` (named apart from Better Auth's `accounts`; the code shape, class, statement line and flags as checks; `item_kind`, set exactly on a control account and locked after first use, S04; `CHART_STATEMENT_LINES` equals the contracts' catalogue, a test keeps them equal), `account_required_dimensions`, `account_roles` and the `account_role` enum.
  - `dimensions.ts` (S02): `dimension_types` and `dimension_values` (`VALUE_CONTRACT_KINDS`, `VALUE_EXTERNAL_SOURCES`).
  - `rates.ts` (S02): `rate_pairs` (one orientation per pair: a unique index on the sorted currencies), `exchange_rates` (append-only; one original row per pair, type and date, and each row superseded at most once, so a date's corrections form one chain) and `bulletin_holidays`; `company_settings.bulletin_days_off` holds the weekly days off.
  - `email.ts`: `email_outbox` (platform, ephemeral; owner decision 2026-10-26): the API writes an email in the transaction of its change, the worker sends and deletes it; `dedupe_key` (S05, unique) queues an email at most once, the held digest's company, day and person: a keyed email is kept as sent (`sent_at`, 0030) until the clean-up deletes it two days later, so its key outlasts the send. `OUTBOX_EMAIL_KINDS` equals `EMAIL_KINDS` in contracts (a test keeps them equal).
  - `drafts.ts` (S03, ADR 0005 "drafts are not ledger"): `entry_drafts` (draft kind: `draft` · `submitted` · `posted`, discarded by archiving; `ENTRY_DRAFT_STATES` equals contracts' `DRAFT_STATES`), `entry_draft_lines` (as typed, with the item a line moves or the new item it opens, one or the other, S04; a removed line is archived and its number's row taken back later, since the app role never deletes) and `entry_attachments` (business: on a draft or a posted entry, by storage key `<company_id>/<id>`; `ATTACHMENT_TYPES`). `journal_entries.draft_id` points at its draft, unique, through a typed constraints callback that breaks the import cycle with `ledger.ts`.
  - `ledger.ts` (S03, ADR 0005, 0008, 0012): the append-only ledger (`posting_requests`, `journal_entries` with `sequence`, `previous_hash` and `hash`, `journal_lines` with the three amounts, `line_conversions` with each rate as used and its row, `dimension_sets` keyed by their sorted `type_id:value_id` pairs and `dimension_set_values`) and the ledger state (`ledger_heads`, `entry_number_counters`, `periods`, `period_balances`). `JOURNAL_ENTRY_SOURCES` equals `ENTRY_SOURCES` of `packages/ledger`.
  - `open-items.ts` (S04, ADR 0013): `open_items` (append-only: one per document on a `document` control account, unique by reference; one per client and currency on a `running` one; its kind the account's, its client its dimension set's), `open_item_movements` (append-only: the one movement of each line on a control account, with the line's three amounts, its entry's date and `adjusted`), `open_item_due_dates` (append-only: a document item's due date from a date on) and `open_item_balances` (ledger-state: each item's open amount and carrying amounts, written only by the trigger on movements). `OPEN_ITEM_KINDS`, `OPEN_ITEM_SOURCES`, `OPEN_ITEM_MOVEMENT_KINDS` and `OPEN_ITEM_DUE_DATE_SETTERS` equal contracts' lists (a test keeps them equal).
  - `trust.ts` (S03 rules 19–21): `chain_anchors` (append-only: a company's chain head at the end of a day in Damascus, one per company and day, with the emails that carried it; written by the jobs role) and `company_exports` (queue: queued, then ready or failed once, a ready one expired past its time; one queued per company; `attempts`, the builds started; `COMPANY_EXPORT_STATES` equals contracts' `EXPORT_STATES`). People are kept by id with no key to `users`, as drafts keep them.
  - `inbox.ts` (S05, ADR 0014): `posting_rule_accounts` (business: one account per `posting_slot` and per currency for a payment method's slot; `postingSlot` equals contracts' `POSTING_SLOTS`), `inbound_events` (append-only: each event as received with its payload, its envelope's hash, its document and item key), `inbound_event_states` (queue: held with its reason and detail and the event it waits behind, posted with `posted_on` when moved, recorded, resolved by a manual entry, dismissed; only a held state changes) and `inbound_event_actions` (append-only: every receipt, replay, attempt and action with its actor). `INBOUND_EVENT_STATES`, `INBOUND_HOLD_REASONS`, `INBOUND_EVENT_ACTIONS` and `INBOUND_ACTOR_KINDS` equal contracts' lists (a test keeps them equal). An event ID is unique per company as well as per client (`0028`), so a replay under the client a document type moved to answers the first event (rule 21). `company_settings` gains the payments' tolerance per invoice currency, outside the settings lock.
  - `recognition.ts` (S06, ADR 0015): `recognition_schedules` (append-only: one per deferred invoice line, from its `invoice.issued`, with its pattern and period or milestone, its retainer cycle key, its client, contract and service values at issue and the contract liability account it was deferred to, a postable liability that is not a control account, checked on insert) and `recognition_movements` (append-only: `deferred`, `elapsed`, `released`, …, each describing an entry posted in its own transaction (its entry's `created_at` is `now()`), signed amounts in the three ledgers, a schedule's first movement its one `deferred`; a month releases once (a unique index); a schedule's remaining never below zero in its transaction amount and zero in every ledger with it, at commit; only a `reclassified` movement may have no entry, with zero amounts). `posting_slot` gains the four recognition slots (0031); their template defaults for companies made before S06 are filled by `mapAppendedTemplateSlots()` at the worker's start, since a value added to an enum cannot be used in the migration's one transaction. `open_items_hub_document_key` includes the reference, so a Hub document has one item per reference on an account (a late line's contract assets, S06 rule 7; a milestone's item per done, `<number>/<n>`, rule 13). S06 PR 2: `inbound_events.order_keys` (text[], empty by default; the further item keys of `orderKeysOf()`, rule 17) with a GIN index `(company_id, order_keys)` through the `btree_gin` extension (`0034`, a trusted extension the owner installs). S06 PR 3: `recognition_movements.credit_minor` (0036, nullable): a credit's part of a schedule, its revenue and liability parts together, on a `credited` movement only (positive on a credit, negative on its void; a check), so a schedule's amount net of credits is its line's amount less their sum; the movement kind `adjusted` (0037, with its month): a month released since a credit's or a credit's void's date, recut as of it (owner, 2026-11-11).
  - `api-clients.ts` (S05, ADR 0003, 0006): `api_clients` (platform, business: one company's client, its system, scopes and IP allow-list; never moved to another company or system), `api_client_keys` (platform, business: the prefix and the SHA-256 of the whole key, created active and unused for a client and company not archived, at most two active per client with the client's row locked first, changed only by its use and its revocation, never again once revoked; `archived_at` stays empty) and `document_sources` (company, business: the client of each document type, a client of the same company and not archived while the source is active; moved to another client or archived, never to another type). `CLIENT_SYSTEMS`, `CLIENT_SCOPES` and `SOURCE_DOCUMENT_TYPES` equal contracts' `API_CLIENT_SYSTEMS`, `API_CLIENT_SCOPES` and `DOCUMENT_TYPES` (a test keeps them equal).
  - `reconciliation.ts` (S08, ADR 0017): `reconciliation_reports` (company, append-only: a client's report of a date as received, its hash, event count and the answer it got, `reconciliation_reports_latest_idx` for the date's latest) and `reconciliation_report_events` (company, append-only: the events it lists, keyed by report and Hub's event id); `reconciliation_report_complete()` (deferred, `0044`) holds a report to exactly the events it counts at commit. `api_clients.reconcile_from` (`0043`–`0045`): backfilled from the creation date in Damascus for the clients that post or report, required by `api_clients_reconcile_from` and never cleared (`api_client_reconcile_from_rules()`).
- `src/schema/columns.ts`: the shared column helpers (`id`, `idIsUuidV7`, `companyId`, `timestamptz`, `createdAt`, `updatedAt`, `archivedAt`). Name a column explicitly when the snake_case casing would split it.
- `src/schema/registry.ts`: every table with its owning module, scope (`company`, `platform`) and kind (`business`, `append-only`, `ledger-state`, `queue`, `draft`, `ephemeral`). It imports nothing: the migration guard loads it with Node's type stripping.
- `src/posting/post.ts`: `post(tx, request)`, **the only writer of the ledger** (ADR 0005 invariant 11; the convention test scans every source file for another): inside `withCompany`, the posting request (a replay answers the first entry, another payload is `IDEMPOTENCY_CONFLICT`), the ledger head then the settings `FOR UPDATE`, the manual date (S03 rule 6), the SYP date, the period made open and read `FOR SHARE` (`PERIOD_CLOSED`), the lines checked and built by `packages/ledger` (a manual entry's `manualEntry()` and `balanceEntry()` on the role accounts, a reversal's `manualReversalLines()`), the accounts and books marked as used, the number of the fiscal year of the entry date, the dimension sets, the hash (`chainEntryValue()`), the entry, lines, conversions (none on a reversal: it read no rate), the `entry.posted` audit row and the head. S04: a manual line names its item (`openItemId`, or `newItem` with a reference and a due date; a running account's item is the client's, found or made), each item's balance row is read `FOR UPDATE` after the head and the period, the posting inserts the new items, their opening due dates and one movement per item line, and the audit row lists the items moved; a reversal moves the same items back (`OPEN_ITEM_HAS_SETTLEMENTS` for an opening whose settlements stand) and, when it moves a running item against its balance or across zero, is followed in the same transaction by its exchange-difference entry under the request `<key>:difference` (S04 rule 13), which is never reversed by hand. A request says what to post (`PostingContent`), never posted amounts. `previewPosting(tx, request)` builds the lines `post()` would post (dates, period, chart checks, measurement, differences, rounding, conversions) with no write and no lock, for the preview of a draft; a refusal of one manual line names it in `details.lineNo`, in `post()` too. `src/posting/reads.ts`: `readChain()` (the chain as stored, typed for `chainEntryValue()`), `rebuildPeriodBalances()`, `readPeriodBalances()` and `readLedgerState()` (the head, counters, period balances by period and dimension key, and the anchors, for the integrity check).
- S05 adds three kinds of `PostingContent`: `hub` (a Hub rule's lines, measured as a manual entry's at the rates of `measuredOn`, the event's own date even when the entry posts later, or a `.changed` event's document date, with Hub's rate as the dealt rate; its new items are Hub documents with their own document date and source `hub_event`; a line marked `carriesItems`, an ad spend's payable, carries what the entry's item lines move instead of a new measurement, and is refused on a manual entry), `hub_reversal` (a Hub entry reversed by its event, `hubReversalLines()`; the difference entry that follows it on a running item is measured at `measuredOn`, the event's own date, so a move to the next open period makes no exchange difference; a `.changed` event's reversal, `withinChange`, has none while the reversed entry is still the latest movement on every running item it moves (`movedSince()`): its new state follows as one change, Q13) and `hub_reclassification` (a line moved between services at its amounts). ## Roles
- `mizan_owner` runs migrations and owns every table, function and schema. It cannot bypass RLS either: with forced RLS it sees company rows only inside `withCompany`. Locally and in CI it may create databases, for the test runs. **No runtime connection uses it** (ADR 0008): `createDatabase` refuses it; the deployment checks the same.
- `mizan_app` is what the API and the worker connect as: no `BYPASSRLS`, `NOINHERIT`, only the privileges each table's custom migration grants. It cannot turn row security off (the query then fails), change a policy, trigger or function, empty a table, set `session_replication_role`, create objects or switch role (`roles.test.ts`).
- `mizan_jobs` is the worker's cross-company jobs role (ADR 0003): no `BYPASSRLS`; it reaches across companies only through policies of its own (`TO mizan_jobs`, SELECT or INSERT) on the tables a migration grants it, by column where it reads. Its grants and policies are pinned in `GUARDS.jobs` (`test/conventions.ts`): any other, or one missing, fails the convention test. S03 gave it the first (`0017`): the head columns of `journal_entries`, the anchors, the owners' memberships, their addresses and company names, four outbox columns, and the state columns of `company_exports`. Audited by `log_statement = 'all'` with `log_parameter_max_length = 0` on the role: every statement it runs is in the server log, its values are not; only a superuser can change either.
- `mizan_queue` is the role pg-boss runs as (`apps/worker`). It has no privilege on any `public` table; it holds `USAGE, CREATE` on schema `pgboss`, where pg-boss installs and owns its tables.
- All four exist before the first migration (cluster-wide: `pnpm db:setup-local` locally and in CI; the deployment on servers, which must also set the jobs role's two settings). No role is a member of another: `setup-local` stops if it finds a membership.

## Rules
- Changes go through `/db-migration`. Never `drizzle-kit push`, never edit a generated migration; commit the schema and its migration together; expand, then contract.
- Every table (the convention test, `test/conventions.ts`, lists each rule): in `public`, owned by `mizan_owner`; a UUIDv7 `id` with `idIsUuidV7`; `created_at`; the timestamps of its kind; a registry entry; snake_case names; no `real`, `double precision`, `money` or zoneless `timestamp`; a `*_minor` amount is `bigint` (ADR 0007); every foreign key covered by a full index. No view or materialized view (a materialized view has no RLS of its own), and no rewrite rule on a table (it could redirect or drop a write that no trigger sees). The app role never gets `TRUNCATE` (it ignores RLS), `TRIGGER`, `MAINTAIN`, `REFERENCES` or `DELETE` (archive instead), except `DELETE` on an `ephemeral` table; the queue role and `PUBLIC` get nothing, the jobs role exactly what `GUARDS.jobs` pins. No function is `SECURITY DEFINER`.
- Company tables (scope `company`): `company_id` not null, every secondary index starts with it; foreign keys between company tables are composite, `(company_id, x_id) → (company_id, id)` with `unique (company_id, id)` on the parent, because PostgreSQL checks foreign keys without RLS; the custom migration enables and forces RLS and creates the one permissive policy `company_isolation` with `USING` and `WITH CHECK (company_id = current_company_id())`, revokes `PUBLIC` and grants the app role what it needs. Any other permissive policy is refused (it would be ORed with `company_isolation`), but the jobs role's own, exactly as `GUARDS.jobs` pins them; a restrictive one only narrows and is allowed.
- `current_company_id()` reads only the transaction setting, with no fallback; `refuse_change()` refuses `UPDATE`, `DELETE` and `TRUNCATE` for every role. **Every guard function is pinned** in `GUARDS.functions` (`test/conventions.ts`), so a `CREATE OR REPLACE` that weakens one fails the convention test; every guard trigger is listed in `GUARDS.triggers` with the events it must fire on (S01's settings lock on `UPDATE`; S03 adds the ledger's), and the test fails if it is missing, rebound, conditional, statement-level, on fewer events or no longer deferred.
- No role but the owner creates temporary tables or schemas in the database (`0000_foundation.sql` revokes `TEMPORARY` from `PUBLIC`): a table name is looked up in the session's temporary schema first, so a temporary table named like a guarded table would be read by a guard function in its place. Guard functions also name their tables with the schema (`public.journal_lines`).
- Company data is read and written only inside `withCompany`, with the company from the authenticated session or the API key, never from request input. Outside it the app role sees no company rows and cannot insert any.
- Ledger-state tables (ADR 0012): `created_at` and `updated_at`, no `archived_at`; the app role has `SELECT, INSERT, UPDATE` only; `refuse_delete()` before `DELETE` and `TRUNCATE`, and a pinned guard before `INSERT` and `UPDATE` that allows only what an append does (the head moves by one entry that follows it, a counter by one number, a period is made open, a period balance is written only at trigger depth 2 and only grows). The migration guard refuses `DELETE`, `TRUNCATE` and `MERGE` on them.
- Append-only tables (ADR 0005): no `updated_at` or `archived_at`; a row trigger `BEFORE UPDATE OR DELETE` and a statement trigger `BEFORE TRUNCATE` with `refuse_change()`; no role but the owner holds `UPDATE` or `DELETE`, and the app role has `SELECT, INSERT` only. The migration guard refuses, on them, `UPDATE`, `DELETE`, `TRUNCATE`, `MERGE`, an upsert's `DO UPDATE`, `DROP TABLE`, `RENAME`, `SET SCHEMA`, a dropped column, a type change, a generated column or expression, inheritance and partitions; anywhere, `CREATE OR REPLACE TRIGGER`, `CREATE RULE`, `DROP OWNED` and `ALTER TYPE … RENAME VALUE` (renaming enum values swaps the meaning of stored rows). No table inherits from another or is a partition (the convention test).
- A plpgsql `IF` condition ends at its first `THEN`, so a `CASE` inside one is parenthesised. A constraint trigger is a constraint: its name must not be a constraint's of the table.
- Every trigger is `ENABLE ALWAYS` (ADR 0008): `CREATE TRIGGER`, then `ALTER TABLE … ENABLE ALWAYS TRIGGER …`. A guard trigger has no `WHEN` condition and no `UPDATE OF` column list. A check that spans rows is a constraint trigger `AFTER … FOR EACH ROW`, `DEFERRABLE INITIALLY DEFERRED`, that checks the whole entry idempotently. `fixture_entry_balances()` is the pattern: the entry header states its line count, so a line added by a later transaction fails the check (a posted entry never changes); an entry the check cannot see fails; tables are schema-qualified. `sum()` skips NULLs, so a check that sums a nullable amount also counts the lines where it is NULL. A constraint trigger cannot be dropped with `DROP CONSTRAINT` (PostgreSQL requires `DROP TRIGGER`, which the guard refuses; `invariants.test.ts`). Locks are taken in one order: the company ledger head, then the period.
- A business table whose deletion would undo a guard (`company_settings`, whose row the settings lock protects) also gets `refuse_delete()` triggers before `DELETE` (row) and `TRUNCATE` (statement), `ENABLE ALWAYS`, listed in `GUARDS.triggers`.
- Ephemeral tables (S01): sessions, one-time tokens, two-factor secrets and rate-limit counters, which Better Auth deletes when they end, are used or expire, and the email outbox, whose rows the worker deletes once sent. Platform scope only, `created_at` and `updated_at` without `archived_at`, the app role holds `SELECT, INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE`, and no foreign key references one (the audit logs keep their ids without one). No company or ledger data is ever ephemeral.
- Money columns (ADR 0007): `bigint` minor units (`bigint({ mode: 'bigint' })`) beside a currency column; rates as `numeric` with their base, quote and type. Never a float.

## Local database
1. `docker compose up -d` at the repository root: PostgreSQL 17 on `127.0.0.1:55417`, a disposable container (`docker compose down -v` disposes of it).
2. Copy `.env.example` to `.env` at the repository root (its fake passwords match the container).
3. `pnpm db:setup-local` (creates or updates the roles and the `vertex_mizan` database; never drops anything), then `pnpm db:migrate`.

The tests need the admin, owner, app, jobs and queue URLs: each run creates `mizan_test_<time>_<random>` as the owner, migrates it, adds the fixture tables and drops it. The superuser URL reaches that database only, for what only a superuser can try (a replica session, role attributes). Turborepo passes only the URLs listed in `passThroughEnv` of the `test` task in `turbo.json`.

Run: `pnpm verify packages/db/test/<file>.test.ts` (some files) · `pnpm --filter @vertex-mizan/db test` (the package).
